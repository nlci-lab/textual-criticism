𝔓⁶⁶ is a small book made of papyrus sheets folded and sewn together, a *codex*, not a scroll. It holds most of John's Gospel. It came to light in Egypt in the 1950s and is now kept mainly at the Bodmer Library near Geneva. Victor Martin published it in 1956. He published fragments of 46 more pages in 1958, and a corrected edition of these followed in 1962 (Metzger and Ehrman, pp. 56–57).

The scribe made many mistakes and corrected many of them, often while still copying. For a text critic the corrections are useful, because they show a copyist in the second or third century comparing readings and choosing between them.

When the UBS committee meets 𝔓⁶⁶ together with 𝔓⁷⁵ and Codex Vaticanus (B), it takes the agreement seriously, because it shows that a reading was already in circulation more than a century before B was copied. Not all scholars give this agreement the same weight; some think the committee relies too much on Egyptian witnesses.
